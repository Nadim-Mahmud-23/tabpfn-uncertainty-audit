# Suggested Reviewers

Five suggestions below, all active researchers in conformal prediction, calibration, or
uncertainty quantification — the core areas of the manuscript and of IJAR.

> IMPORTANT, before entering these in Editorial Manager:
> 1. **Look up and verify each reviewer's current institutional email** on their official
>    university/lab page. Emails are deliberately not pre-filled here, because they change
>    and should not be guessed.
> 2. **Check for conflicts of interest.** Avoid anyone you have co-authored with recently,
>    shared an institution with, or otherwise have a close relationship to. Reviewers 3-5
>    below are cited in the paper; suggesting cited authors is common and generally
>    acceptable, but confirm there is no co-authorship/advisor conflict before listing them.
> 3. Most editors want 3-5; pick the subset you are most comfortable with.

---

1. **Henrik Boström** — KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden.
   Expertise: conformal prediction methodology, Mondrian/label-conditional conformal
   classifiers, calibration. Long-standing contributor to the conformal-prediction
   community that IJAR regularly publishes. Strong fit for the methodology and the
   group-conditional (Mondrian) focus.

2. **Tuwe Löfström** — Jönköping University, Jönköping, Sweden.
   Expertise: conformal prediction, calibration, and conformal classifiers on imbalanced /
   small subgroups. Directly relevant to the paper's small-stratum failure regime; his work
   on bias-reduction for sparse Mondrian categories is cited in the manuscript.

3. **Matteo Sesia** — University of Southern California, Los Angeles, USA.
   Expertise: conformal prediction, Adaptive Prediction Sets (APS), conditional coverage and
   conformal fairness. Co-author of the APS method discussed in Sections 3 and 6. (Cited.)

4. **Anastasios N. Angelopoulos** — University of California, Berkeley / Stanford, USA.
   Expertise: conformal prediction foundations, class-conditional conformal prediction, and
   the distributional (Beta) law of split-conformal coverage that the paper's null model
   builds on. (Cited.)

5. **Eyke Hüllermeier** — LMU Munich, Germany.
   Expertise: uncertainty quantification in machine learning (aleatoric vs. epistemic
   uncertainty), probabilistic prediction, and reliable ML — squarely within IJAR's scope.
   Provides a complementary, non-conformal-specialist perspective on the uncertainty claims.
